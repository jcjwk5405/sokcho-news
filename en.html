Editor's Letter

Why Is One Maternity Ward So Hard to Keep Open?

This week's editorial and debate topic ask the same question from two sides. The editorial looks at why Sokcho still has no maternity ward, and suggests that the four Seorak counties could solve this together by sharing emergency medical services. Our debate topic asks the bigger version of that question: should Korea allow private health insurance to compete alongside the national health insurance system?

Nationally, police faced heavy criticism this week after officers were found to have falsely closed a missing-person case in Jeju as a traffic death, when the person had in fact been missing for months. President Lee Jae-myung ordered police reform on August 28. The same week, the presidential office asked the chief justice to renominate a Supreme Court justice candidate, leading to a public clash between the two offices. The central bank also raised its policy rate for a second straight time, to 3.0%.

This week's opinion section combines two polls, Gallup and the National Barometer Survey (NBS). Gallup found the president's approval rating at 42%, its fourth straight weekly drop, with disapproval reaching a new high of 50%. Housing policy remained the top reason people gave for disapproving in both surveys.

County Notices

Summer Ends, Preparations for Autumn Begin

🏙️ Sokcho

💰 Citizen budget proposals

Sokcho is accepting citizen proposals for its 2027 participatory budget through September 15. Any resident can suggest a project, and selected ideas will be included in next year's city budget.

🥾 Hiking reservations expanding

Seoraksan National Park will expand its hiking reservation system in September ahead of the autumn foliage season. Visitors are encouraged to book ahead, since some trail sections may turn away hikers without a reservation.

🕊️ Goseong

🎒 School-route safety checks

Goseong is inspecting the routes children walk to school ahead of the new semester, looking for broken pavement, poor lighting, and other hazards.

🕊 Peace trail application

Goseong has applied to have its DMZ border-area peace trail designated a national trail. If approved, the county would receive national funding to improve the path and its signage.

🏄 Yangyang

🏖 Beach season ends

Yangyang's beaches ended their summer lifeguard season in late August. Swimming afterward is not recommended, since safety staff are no longer on duty.

🚀 Youth start-up support

Yangyang is accepting applications through September 10 for its second round of start-up support for young entrepreneurs, covering rent and early business costs.

⛰️ Inje

🌲 Fire watch volunteers

Inje is recruiting volunteers to watch for wildfires ahead of the autumn fire season. Duties include monitoring the forest and helping with early response.

🤝 Joint disaster drill

Inje County and the Army's 12th Division held a joint civilian-military disaster drill on August 28, preparing for landslides and flooding ahead of typhoon season.

This Week's Terms

Words in the News, Explained

Three terms that came up often in this week's national newspaper editorials — explained simply, so you can follow the news even if you're new to Korean politics and policy.

RenominationLAW

When the president withdraws a Supreme Court justice candidate's approval request from the National Assembly and asks the chief justice to nominate someone else instead. This week, the presidential office made this request, leading to a public clash with the chief justice.

False ClosurePOLICE

When an officer marks a case as resolved in police records without actually resolving it. A missing-person case in Jeju was falsely closed this way, which is what triggered this week's calls for police reform.

Acting DirectorGOVERNMENT

Someone who temporarily performs the duties of a position that is vacant or awaiting a permanent appointment. The acting national police chief apologized for this week's case, which also raised questions about a leadership gap at the top of the police force.
