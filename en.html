Editor's Letter

Worries Inside and Out

This week, Nvidia's CEO Jensen Huang stepped down, and the finance chief became the interim CEO. Korea's Fair Trade Commission fined two food delivery apps more than 630 billion won in total. More than 20 million foreign tourists have visited Korea this year, the first time ever. The college entrance exam is on November 19.

Four of the five national newspapers wrote about the special counsel bill that the National Assembly passed. Most of them worried that it could lead to dropping court cases related to the president. But the polls were close. In the Gallup Korea poll, 44% supported the special counsel and 37% opposed it. In the NBS poll, 41% said passing the bill was a good thing and 39% said it was not. In both polls, about one in five people were undecided. The president's approval rating in Gallup fell from 41% to 39%, while support for the ruling Democratic Party rose from 36% to 39%.

The old saying we chose this week means "worries inside and troubles outside." This week's editorial says that cooperation among the four Seorak cities and counties needs a written agreement, and that citizens should help write it in the open. In the region, Daepo Port in Sokcho was chosen first in the country to become a national fishing port hub, with a 95 billion won project. Inje will start an on-demand bus in Sangnam-myeon at the end of this month.

County Notices

Water Supply Work, a Youth Center, Good-Price Shops, and Online English Classes

🏙️ Sokcho

🚰 Apply for water supply work by November 27

Sokcho will stop private water supply work from December 7 to March 1 next year because of winter cold. If you need this work done this year, apply by November 27. Ask the city's water office for details. Sokcho also runs a walking challenge on the Walkon app until November 29. Walk 6,000 steps or more between 5 p.m. and 9 p.m. Fifteen people will win a 10,000-won gift voucher. Questions: Sokcho City Hall, 033-639-2114.

🕊️ Goseong

🏠 Rooms for young people at a new youth center

Goseong is looking for people to use six rooms at a new youth center in Bongpo-ri, Toseong-myeon. People aged 18 to 45 can apply, and they can stay for three years. Young people without a job pay half the fee. The county is also asking for ideas for the center's name, with prizes of up to 300,000 won. Check the county notice for the deadline. Questions: Goseong County Office, Jobs Team, 033-680-3738.

🏄 Yangyang

🏷 Good-price shops: apply November 16 to 20

Yangyang is choosing new "good-price shops" that keep prices low, such as restaurants, hair salons, and laundries. Shop owners can apply in person at the county office from November 16 to 20. The county will visit the shops and announce its choices in December. Questions: Yangyang County Office, 033-670-2114.

⛰️ Inje

📚 Online English classes for students, until November 19

Inje will help pay for students' online English classes with native speakers next year. It is for 180 elementary, middle, and high school students and young people not in school. If the class costs less than 100,000 won a month, the county pays all of it; if it costs more, the county pays 80%. Apply at lifelong.inje.go.kr until November 19. Questions: Inje County Office, 033-461-2122.

This Week's Terms

Words in the News, Explained

Three terms that came up often in this week's national newspaper editorials — explained simply, so you can follow the news even if you're new to Korean politics and policy.

Withdrawing an indictmentLAW

When a prosecutor takes back a criminal case that is already in court. In Korea, this can only be done before the first trial court gives its ruling. Then the court ends the case. Newspapers discussed it this week in editorials about the special counsel bill and the new justice minister nominee.

Price-to-book ratio (PBR)MARKETS

A company's stock price divided by its net assets per share. If it is below 1, the market values the company at less than what it owns. The ruling party wants to use this number in a new inheritance tax rule, and newspapers disagreed about it.

Most-favored-nation (MFN) demandFAIR TRADE

When a company asks its partners to give it terms at least as good as the terms they give anyone else. The Fair Trade Commission said two delivery apps made this demand of restaurants and fined them.
