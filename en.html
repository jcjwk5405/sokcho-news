Editor's Letter

Working Together Brings More Government Funding

This week's editorial makes a simple point: when Sokcho, Goseong, Yangyang, and Inje apply for national government funding together instead of alone, they get better terms and a higher chance of approval. Our debate topic asks a related question: should the law force companies and people to spread out from the Seoul metropolitan area, or should the market decide on its own?

Nationally, disagreement over the government's new real estate tax plan continued this week. President Lee's approval rating fell to a six-month low in both the Gallup poll (44%) and the NBS survey (49%). A joint Korea-US military exercise is about to begin, and debate over the timing of transferring wartime operational control back to Korea has flared up again.

This week's opinion section returns to using the Gallup Korea Daily Opinion poll, alongside the NBS national survey published the same week — so readers can compare both results side by side.

County Notices

Deadlines, Funding Drives, and Peak Summer

🏙️ Sokcho

💰 Relief payment deadline

Sokcho's livelihood relief payment (200,000 won per resident) closes on September 11. Anyone who has not applied yet can do so online or at their local community center, no set day required.

🎨 New cultural space

The city is gathering resident input on the design of a new 400-million-dollar arts belt along Cheongcho Lake, including a 500-seat waterside performance hall.

🕊️ Goseong

🕊 Peace economic zone

Goseong is competing for national funding to designate a 1.6-trillion-won peace economic zone near Hwajinpo. Officials are considering a joint application with neighboring counties, since projects submitted together tend to receive more government money.

🌡 Heat wave notice

Cooling centers at senior centers and community halls remain open through mid-August. Anyone showing signs of heat illness should call 119 right away.

🏄 Yangyang

🏖 Peak beach season

Naksan and Jukdo beaches are busy even on weekdays as Yangyang's summer season peaks. County officials say more visitors are staying overnight instead of just passing through, which helps local businesses.

📋 Policy review meeting

The county held a briefing on its 50 campaign-pledge projects across five policy areas, with a full progress report to be posted on the county website.

⛰️ Inje

🏔 Regional fund secured

Inje secured 2 billion won over five years from the national fund for shrinking regions, and is now working with neighboring border counties on joint projects.

🌡 Heat wave notice

Cooling centers in mountain villages remain open through mid-August. Anyone showing signs of heat illness should call 119 right away.

This Week's Terms

Words in the News, Explained

Three terms that came up often in this week's national newspaper editorials — explained simply, so you can follow the news even if you're new to Korean politics and policy.

Mega Special ZoneECONOMY

A plan to relax the 52-hour workweek limit for advanced industries like semiconductors, inside special economic zones. This week's editorials said the presidential office has not settled disagreements between government ministries on how it would work.

Wartime Operational Control (OPCON)SECURITY

The authority to command Korean forces during a war. In peacetime, Korea holds this authority; in wartime, it currently passes to the commander of the Korea-US Combined Forces. With joint military exercises about to begin, debate over when to fully transfer this authority back to Korea has resumed.

"Yellow Envelope" Labor LawLABOR

A law that limits how much companies can sue striking workers for damages. This week's editorials focused on a dispute over the government's rules for which strikes qualify for this protection.
