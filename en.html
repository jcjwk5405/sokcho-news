Editor's Letter

Rushing Rarely Gets You There

This week, all four Seorak-area local governments — Sokcho, Goseong, Yangyang, and Inje — began new four-year terms on July 1. From this issue on, we will track how well these four leaders keep their campaign promises.

National newspapers this week covered two connected stories: a large national investment plan, and a contentious process over assigning parliamentary committee seats. Confucius once said, "rushing leads to failure, not success" — a lesson that applies just as much to trillion-won national projects as it does to local youth policy. Getting it right matters more than getting it fast.

This issue also asks what young people need to put down roots here (jobs, housing, and community), and whether elementary schools should ban smartphones. Different questions, but both about how the next generation grows up and stays in this region.

County Notices

What Changed on July 1

🏙️ Sokcho

🏛 Inauguration

Mayor Lee Byung-sun was sworn in on July 1 for his 9th-term term as Mayor of Sokcho. He skipped a formal transition committee, choosing to keep existing city policies running without interruption.

💰 Benefit for residents

Mayor Lee's top campaign promise — a livelihood recovery grant — will be paid starting July 20.

Every Sokcho resident, regardless of income, assets, or age, will receive ₩200,000 as a local gift certificate ("Sokcho Sarang Sangpumgwon"), usable at shops across the city.

🕊️ Goseong

🏛 Inauguration

County Chief Ham Myung-jun was sworn in on July 1 for his third term. Given tight local finances, the ceremony was kept small, without outside guests.

📰 Community

NH Nonghyup's Goseong branch donated ₩2 million to the county's hometown-giving fund. On July 3, Goseong County hosted an international forum on branding the region's "peace resources" (its location near the DMZ).

🏄 Yangyang

🏛 Inauguration

County Chief Kim Jung-joong was sworn in on July 1 — the first Democratic Party county chief in Yangyang's history. He named restoring public trust in local government as his top priority, and ordered a special task force on livelihood stability and administrative reform.

⛰️ Inje

🏛 Inauguration

County Chief Choi Sang-gi was sworn in on July 1 for his third term. His first official act was paying respects at the county's war memorial.

💧 Local issue

Chief Choi raised concerns that Han River Basin Fund support to Inje — long around ₩30 billion a year — is expected to fall sharply to about ₩6.7 billion next year, and called for a fairer funding split between the province and its counties.

This Week's Terms

Words in the News, Explained

Three terms that came up often in this week's national newspaper editorials — explained simply, so you can follow the news even if you're new to Korean politics and policy.

Committee AssignmentPOLITICS

When a new National Assembly term begins, lawmakers must decide who chairs each standing committee and who sits on it. When the ruling and opposition parties can't agree, the majority party sometimes pushes the assignments through alone — which is exactly what sparked conflict again this week.

Special ProsecutorLAW

An independent investigator specially appointed by the National Assembly to handle cases where the regular prosecution service can't investigate fairly — often because the case involves politically powerful people. Whether to appoint one is frequently a point of dispute between parties.

Land Transaction Permit ZoneECONOMY

An area designated by the government to curb real-estate speculation. To buy property inside one, you must prove you genuinely intend to live there or use it for business, and get official permission first. Restricting one area often just pushes speculative buying to a nearby one instead — known as the "balloon effect."
