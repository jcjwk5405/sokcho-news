Editor's Letter

A View of Mt. Geumgang, and a Question About History

This week's editorial is about Goseong's DMZ — the only place in Korea where the public can see North Korea's Mt. Geumgang from a viewpoint. The editorial argues that Goseong cannot turn this into a real tourism draw alone. It needs help from all four Seorak counties. Timed for Liberation Day on August 15, our debate topic this week asks whether Korea should bring back a single, government-approved history textbook.

Nationally, the National Assembly passed a bill that ends prosecutors' power to investigate crimes on their own — a system that had lasted 72 years. President Lee Jae-myung did not veto the bill, but he warned police to use their new power carefully. A few days later, the president told his own government to rework a tax reform plan after investors and members of his own party pushed back. This week's featured Chinese saying, roughly "give an order in the morning, change it by evening," fits that mood — several government plans changed direction fast this week. Seoul also hit 40°C for the first time in eight years, and one city in Gyeongnam recorded Korea's hottest temperature in 122 years.

A new Gallup poll won't come out until August 14. So this week we're using the same poll numbers as last week: 53% approval for the president, the lowest since he took office.

This Week's Terms

Words in the News, Explained

Three terms that came up often in this week's national newspaper editorials — explained simply, so you can follow the news even if you're new to Korean politics and policy.

Public Prosecutors' OfficeLAW

A new agency that will handle only prosecution — deciding whether to charge someone in court — once Korea's old system of combined police-and-prosecutor investigation power ends on October 2. Police will now do almost all criminal investigations on their own.

ISAECONOMY

Short for Individual Savings Account — a Korean account that lets people save and invest with tax benefits. This week, the president ordered a full rework of planned ISA changes after investors and young savers pushed back.

Extended DeterrenceDEFENSE

Also called the "nuclear umbrella" — the US promise to respond with its own nuclear weapons if an ally like South Korea is attacked with nuclear weapons. It's back in the news after reports that the US is considering a new strategy that leans more on short-range nuclear weapons.
