Editor's Letter

Troubles Inside the Walls

This week, the KOSPI stock index fell more than 7% in one day, and trading stopped for 20 minutes. The first head of the new Serious Crimes Investigation Agency was also appointed. All five national newspapers wrote about both events. They also wrote about a dispute between Korea and the U.S. over sharing intelligence, and about the National Pension Fund losing value in the market fall.

In Gallup Korea's new poll, 39% approve of the president's job performance and 52% disapprove. In the NBS poll, it is 44% and 49%. Among those who disapprove, 12% named the economy or the stock market as the main reason. That is 5 points more than in the last poll. The topics that fill the newspapers are not always the reasons people give in polls, but this week the two met on the economy.

The saying we chose this week means "the trouble is inside the walls." Many of this week's problems came from inside — arguments inside the ruling party, and the government and Seoul City blaming each other over housing. Our question that nobody asked this week is simple: "Will my own pension go down?" By law, the state guarantees that pensions are paid, and you can check your expected pension on the National Pension Service app or by calling 1355.

This week's editorial says that when a city promises 1,000 jobs for young people, what kind of jobs they are matters more than the number. In Sokcho, the number of businesses started by young people rose 4.0% to 6,115 in the first half of the year. In the region, new resident councils started on October 15 under the law. From January 2027, Inje County's rural and village buses will be free.

County Notices

Bridge Repairs, Free Tax Advice, a Video Contest, and Free Buses

🏙️ Sokcho

🚧 Geumho Bridge repairs until late November

Repair work has started on Geumho Bridge, which opened in 1994. It should finish in late November. Some lanes will be closed during the work, but cars can still cross. Please drive slowly near the work area. Questions: Sokcho City Hall, 033-639-2114.

💳 Unpaid local taxes — payment plans for people in hardship

Until the end of the year, Sokcho is collecting unpaid local taxes and fees, such as property tax and car tax. If you owe a small amount and are going through hard times, you may be able to pay in installments. You can pay at a bank ATM, on Wetax (wetax.go.kr), or by card at the city hall tax office. Questions: Sokcho City Hall, 033-639-2114.

🕊️ Goseong

🧾 Free tax advice on October 27

Do you have a question about income tax, inheritance tax, or property tax? A local tax accountant will give free one-to-one advice on October 27, from 1 to 3 p.m., in the meeting room on the 3rd floor of the Goseong County Office new building. Early sign-up has closed, but you can still visit on the day. Questions: Goseong County Tax Division, 033-680-3282.

🏄 Yangyang

🎬 Video contest until November 12

Show what you love about Yangyang — its festivals, places to visit, or county life. There are two types: a regular video (horizontal, up to 2 minutes) and a short video (vertical, up to 1 minute). Fourteen winners will share 10 million won in prizes. Check the Yangyang County website for who can enter and how. Questions: Yangyang County Office, 033-670-2114.

🎉 Town festivals on October 21–23

Each town in Yangyang holds a festival for its residents: Hyeonbuk-myeon on the 21st, Yangyang-eup and Sonyang-myeon on the 22nd, and Seo-myeon and Hyeonnam-myeon on the 23rd. Ask your local community center for the time and place. Questions: Yangyang County Office, 033-670-2114.

⛰️ Inje

🚌 Free rural and village buses from January 2027

From January 1, 2027, anyone — residents and visitors — can ride Inje's regular rural buses and village buses for free. You still need to tap your transit card when you get on, so the county can count riders. The Inje city tour bus is not free. Questions: Inje County Office, 033-461-2122.

This Week's Terms

Words in the News, Explained

Three terms that came up often in this week's national newspaper editorials — explained simply, so you can follow the news even if you're new to Korean politics and policy.

Circuit BreakerSTOCK MARKET

A rule that stops all stock trading for a short time when prices fall too fast. In Korea, if the index stays 8% or more below the day before for one minute, trading stops for 20 minutes. It happened on both the KOSPI and KOSDAQ on October 15.

RebalancingPENSION

Bringing an investment mix back to its target share. If stocks go up and become too large a part, some are sold; if they fall, some are bought. This year the National Pension Fund delayed selling Korean stocks and raised its target. After this week's fall, newspapers debated that choice.

Appearance of FairnessLAW

The idea that public bodies such as courts and investigators must not only be fair but also look fair. This week, papers used it when they discussed whether the heads of the new investigation and prosecution agencies should regularly attend Cabinet meetings.
