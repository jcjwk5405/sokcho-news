Editor's Letter

Reading the Season from a Single Leaf

This week, the Democratic Party won both houses of the U.S. Congress in the midterm elections. On November 6, Korea's main stock index, the KOSPI, fell more than 6% and briefly dropped below 5,000. The National Assembly passed a special counsel bill. The government decided to send the Cheonghae naval unit to a wider area south of Oman, and it plans to delay the tax on crypto income for two more years.

All five national newspapers wrote about the U.S. election, the stock market, the special counsel bill, and the naval unit. But the new Gallup Korea poll did not ask about those topics. In the poll, 41% approve of the president's job performance and 50% disapprove. Gallup also asked about the new criminal justice agencies that started a month ago. Only 29% trust the new agency for serious crimes, and 25% trust the new prosecution office. About one in four people had no opinion yet.

The old saying we chose this week means that one falling leaf tells you autumn has come. Many newspapers said we should read small signs that the chip boom may slow down. This week's editorial asks how a tourist tax could pay for costs that tourists leave behind, such as traffic, worn roads, and trash. In the region, Goseong applied for a rural basic income program that would give each resident 150,000 won a month. Sokcho has had no wildfires for seven years. Please do not burn anything near forests until December 15.

County Notices

A Clay Walking Path, Bird-Watching Classes, Land Price Appeals, and Card Fee Support

🏙️ Sokcho

🚶 Indoor barefoot clay path opens for winter

Sokcho opened a barefoot walking path inside a greenhouse at the city flower nursery on November 2. It takes the place of the lakeside paths at Yeongnangho and Cheongchoho, which close in winter. The clay path was made 30 meters longer this year, and there is a hot-water foot bath. It is open from 9 a.m. to 6 p.m. in November, and until 9 p.m. from December. Anyone can use it. Questions: Sokcho City Hall, 033-639-2114.

🕊️ Goseong

🐦 Bird-watching village school until December 6

Goseong runs nature classes for residents near Songjiho Lake until December 6. There are talks about birds and sand dune plants, a family forest walk, and crafts like bird cakes. Sign up for each class at litt.ly/nanaplay. Questions: Goseong County Office, Education and Culture Division, 033-680-4182.

🏄 Yangyang

🗺️ Land price appeals until November 27

Yangyang announced official land prices for 750 lots that were split, joined, or changed in use in the first half of this year. If you disagree with a price, you can file an appeal by November 27. Ask the county office how to apply. Questions: Yangyang County Office, 033-670-2114.

⛰️ Inje

💳 Card fee support for local shops, until November 27

Inje is taking more applications for card fee support from shops that accept the Inje Chaewo Dream Card. It is for shops that missed the first round in March and April. The amount depends on the shop's card sales in 2025 and its yearly sales. Ask the county office how to apply. Questions: Inje County Office, 033-461-2122.

This Week's Terms

Words in the News, Explained

Three terms that came up often in this week's national newspaper editorials — explained simply, so you can follow the news even if you're new to Korean politics and policy.

Circuit breakerMARKETS

A rule that stops all stock trading for a short time when prices fall very fast. In Korea, if the KOSPI is down 8% or more from the day before for one minute, trading stops for 20 minutes. It happened on November 6, three minutes after the market opened.

Future Response FundBUDGET

A new government fund of about 162 trillion won planned for next year. The government expects extra tax money from the chip boom, and it wants to keep this money in a fund instead of spending it all at once. Some will go to youth, new industries, regions, and education, and the rest will be kept as a reserve. Newspapers argued this week about how it should be used and controlled.

Separation of religion and politicsLAW

A principle in Korea's Constitution. Article 20 says there is no state religion and that religion and politics are separate. Newspapers discussed it this week after reports that many members of one religious group had joined a political party.
