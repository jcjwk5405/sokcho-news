Editor's Letter

What Makes Us Special, and How Should We Show It?

This week's editorial and debate topic ask a similar question from different angles. The editorial argues that Sokcho, Goseong, Yangyang, and Inje should build their tourism identity around local stories — like the displaced-family history of Sokcho's Abai Village, Goseong's DMZ observatory, Yangyang's surfing culture, and Inje's mountain traditions — instead of generic cafes and gift shops. Our debate topic asks a related question: should the historic Gwanghwamun gate in Seoul add a Hangeul sign next to its existing Chinese-character sign?

Nationally, the government's August 30 cabinet reshuffle kept making headlines this week. Justice minister nominee Kim Seung-won faced allegations over a past case and personal finances, and gender equality minister nominee Yong Hye-in said she would keep her National Assembly seat even after becoming a minister, drawing criticism from lawmakers across parties. Presidential policy chief Kim Yong-beom resigned on September 1, a day after being kept in his post during the reshuffle. The government also submitted an 820.9-trillion-won budget for 2027 to the National Assembly, and a data breach at streaming service TVING exposed about 40 million user accounts.

This week's opinion section covers Gallup Korea's latest survey. President Lee Jae-myung's approval rating fell to 40%, its lowest point since March, while disapproval rose to 51%, a new high. The reason for disapproval that jumped the most this week was "personnel appointments," up 8 percentage points — a sign that this week's cabinet controversy is already showing up in the numbers.

County Notices

Welfare Plans, a Beef Festival, and a Library Reading Month

🏙️ Sokcho

📋 Welfare plan public hearing

Sokcho held a public hearing on August 28 for its 2027–2030 community welfare plan, setting out the city's welfare policy direction for the next four years. The plan will be finalized by the end of September.

🎟 Group tourist incentive program

Sokcho announced a group-tourist incentive program for the second half of the year, offering rewards to travel agencies and organizations that bring in large tour groups during the slower fall and winter season.

🕊️ Goseong

🕊 DMZ Road & Peace Festa

Goseong confirmed its program for the "DMZ Road & Peace DMZ Festa," building on last month's application to have its DMZ border-area peace trail designated a national trail.

🐄 Hanwoo beef festival

Goseong will hold its "2026 Clean Goseong Hanwoo Festival" from September 11 to 13 at Bongsudae Beach, featuring Korean beef, local farm products, and performances by the shore.

🏄 Yangyang

🏆 Special award from the province

Yangyang received a special award in Gangwon's 2026 joint government evaluation of counties, along with about 40 million won in incentive funding to be used for resident services.

⛰️ Inje

🏘 Apartment facility support

Inje is offering additional funding to improve aging facilities — roads, walkways, and community halls — in 78 apartment complexes with a total of 1,940 households.

📚 Reading Month at the library

Inje's six public libraries are running a "Reading September" program from September 5 to 30, expanding borrowing limits from 5 to 10 books per person for the month.

This Week's Terms

Words in the News, Explained

Three terms that came up often in this week's national newspaper editorials — explained simply, so you can follow the news even if you're new to Korean politics and policy.

Prosecutorial SuspensionLAW

A decision by prosecutors to acknowledge that a crime took place but not send the case to trial, taking the circumstances into account. This term came up repeatedly this week over allegations facing the new justice minister nominee.

Dual Office-HoldingPOLITICS

Holding two official positions at the same time. This week, the gender equality minister nominee's plan to keep her National Assembly seat while serving as minister sparked criticism from lawmakers across parties.

Proportional RepresentativeGOVERNMENT

A lawmaker elected based on a party's share of the national vote, rather than from a specific district. Because these seats are tied closely to a party's status in the National Assembly, this fact became central to this week's dual office-holding debate.
