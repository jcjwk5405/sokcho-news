Editor's Letter

Think of Danger in Times of Peace

This week, all five national newspapers wrote about the same five topics. They covered the AI-assisted hacking of banks and North Korea's landmines in the DMZ. They also covered tension between Korea and the U.S. and the new system for criminal cases. The fifth topic was the government stepping back on farmland checks and a Pacific trade deal. Gallup Korea is back after the Chuseok break. In its new poll, 37% approve of the president's job performance and 51% disapprove. Among those who disapprove, 15% named defense, security, or North Korea as the main reason. That is 12 points more than in mid-September.

The saying we chose this week means "think of danger in times of peace." This week, many problems came to light only after something went wrong. The papers disagreed on solutions, but they agreed we should have prepared earlier.

This week's editorial asks how much of the 648.4 billion won that visitors spent in Sokcho last year stayed in our community. In the region, the government again listed Goseong and Yangyang as areas of population decline. Sokcho and Inje were placed in a group called "population vitality plus," and Inje County says it is unhappy with the decision. People in Sokcho and Goseong also disagree about a planned apartment complex in front of Ulsanbawi Rock.

County Notices

Land Survey Help, Pet Registration, and Support for Families

🏙️ Sokcho

📐 Help desk for a land boundary survey

Do you own land in the Cheonghak 2 area? The city is checking land boundaries there. Until October 23, a help desk is open on the second floor of the Gyodong Community Center. You can get information and agree on your land's boundary there. Questions: Sokcho City Hall, Civil Affairs and Land Division, 033-639-2114.

🕊️ Goseong

🐶 Register your pet without a fine

If you keep a pet two months or older at home and have not registered it, you can do it by October 31 without paying a fine. Get a microchip or a tag collar at an animal hospital, and hand in the form there. Checks will start in November. Questions: Goseong Animal Protection Center, 033-680-3722.

🏄 Yangyang

🧺 Free food and daily goods for people in hardship

If money is tight, or you have lost a job or closed a business, you can get a set of food and daily goods. Visit the Supia Laundry self-support business at 3-8 Hyeonsangongwon-gil, Yangyang-eup, from Tuesday to Thursday, 2 to 5 p.m. You can get help once a month, up to three times a year. Staff can also connect you with other support. Questions: Yangyang County Office, 033-670-2114.

📝 Health plan survey until October 14

Yangyang is asking residents about health care for the next four years. Any resident can take part online or on paper. Questions: Yangyang County Office, 033-670-2114.

⛰️ Inje

👶 More help with postpartum care costs

For babies born on or after January 1, 2027, Inje County will pay up to 1.5 million won per mother for postpartum care, up from 500,000 won. Both parents must have lived in Inje for the six months before the birth. Apply within six months of the birth at the county health center, on Gov24, or by registered mail. Keep your receipts. Questions: Inje County Office, 033-461-2122.

This Week's Terms

Words in the News, Explained

Three terms that came up often in this week's national newspaper editorials — explained simply, so you can follow the news even if you're new to Korean politics and policy.

Network SeparationFINANCE

A security rule that keeps a bank's main computer network apart from the internet. Korea made it strict after a big cyberattack in 2013. There has been talk of easing the rule to use AI. After this week's hacking, some say Korea should slow down.

Competence Dispute RulingLAW

A case in which the Constitutional Court decides which public body holds a certain power. This week, the president and the chief justice disagreed about a Supreme Court nomination. One newspaper said they could ask the court to decide if talks fail.

CPTPPTRADE

A free trade agreement among 12 countries, including Japan, the UK, Canada, and Australia. It cuts many tariffs and opens markets widely. That can help factories but may hurt farming and fishing. This week, the government decided not to apply to join this year.
