Editor's Letter

Keeping Promises Builds Trust

This week, South Korea and the United States postponed their yearly defense meeting, called the SCM, until early next year. The two sides still disagree on when Korea should take wartime control of its own troops. Hackers also attacked more financial companies, including a securities firm and a crypto exchange. The economy grew 1.0% in the third quarter, much faster than expected. But household spending fell a little.

All five national newspapers wrote about the delayed meeting, the hacking, and North Korean leader Kim Jong Un's visit to China. In Gallup Korea's new poll, 39% approve of the president's job performance and 49% disapprove. Another poll, NBS, found 46% and 44%. The two polls moved in different directions on party support, so one poll alone is not enough. The old saying we chose this week tells of a ruler who kept a small promise to win people's trust.

This week's editorial asks residents to take a real part in decisions about tourism in their towns. Tourists bring business, but locals often face more traffic, noise, and trash. In the region, the cost of the Osaek cable car project rose by 62.1 billion won. Inje residents protested in front of the National Assembly. Goseong started special checks against animal diseases until February. Farmers should report any sick animals right away.

County Notices

A Barefoot Walk, Pet Registration, Free Fluoride, and a History Talk

🏙️ Sokcho

🚶 Barefoot walking challenge on November 7

A barefoot walking event will be held in Sokcho on November 7. It opens at Sokcho Beach at 1 p.m. There are four courses, including the beach and clay paths around Cheongchoho Lake and Yeongnangho Lake. Buses take walkers between courses. Ask the organizers how to sign up. Questions: Sokcho City Hall, 033-639-2114.

🕊️ Goseong

🐕 Register your pet — checks start in November

Dogs and other pets older than two months must be registered. The free self-report period ended on October 31, and Goseong will start checks in November. Visit an animal hospital to get a microchip or a tag and fill in a form. You may be fined if your pet is not registered. Questions: Goseong Animal Protection Center, 033-680-3722.

🏄 Yangyang

🦷 Free fluoride mouthwash every Thursday

Until the end of November, the Yangyang Public Health Center gives out free fluoride mouthwash every Thursday afternoon. Go to the oral health room on the 1st floor. Any Yangyang resident of any age can get it. Use one pack once a week after brushing, and do not eat or drink for 30 to 60 minutes. Questions: Yangyang County Office, 033-670-2114.

⛰️ Inje

📚 Korean history talk on November 7

Historian Shim Yong-hwan will give a talk at the Inje Miracle Library on November 7 at 2 p.m. Students, parents, and all residents are welcome. Sign up on the library website. Seats are first come, first served. Questions: Inje County Office, 033-461-2122.

This Week's Terms

Words in the News, Explained

Three terms that came up often in this week's national newspaper editorials — explained simply, so you can follow the news even if you're new to Korean politics and policy.

Security Consultative Meeting (SCM)SECURITY

The top yearly meeting between the defense ministers of South Korea and the United States. They discuss military issues of the alliance and release a joint statement. This year the meeting was pushed to early next year because the two sides disagree on when Korea should take wartime control of its troops. It is the first time in 28 years that the autumn meeting moved to the next year.

Crypto-Asset Reporting Framework (CARF)TAX

An international system made by the OECD. Tax offices in different countries share information about crypto trades made by foreign residents. Korea will start next year, but the United States will join in 2029. The government gave this gap as one reason to delay taxing crypto income again.

DiscoveryLAW

A rule that makes both sides in a lawsuit show each other their evidence before the trial. It is used in the United States. It can help victims of data leaks, because companies usually hold the records. After this week's hacking cases, some people asked Korea to adopt it.
