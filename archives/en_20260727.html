Editor's Letter

The Truth Is Still in the Fog

This week's paper carries two editorials at once. One asks how Seorak's four counties can make sure construction workers get paid on time when big tourism projects break ground. The other asks local councils to keep watching their mayors and county chiefs closely. Our debate topic this week asks a different question: should Korea move to a four-day workweek?

Nationally, the ruling party confirmed a plan to remove a prosecutorial power, and the opposition promised to fight it in parliament. The US also set a new 12.5% tariff on Korean goods this week. In Incheon, a warehouse fire at a Coupang logistics center was finally put out — and then a fire broke out at another Coupang warehouse just a few kilometers away. This week's featured Chinese saying, roughly "lost in a five-mile fog," captures the mood: several stories this week share one thing in common — the full truth still hasn't come out.

A new Gallup poll shows the president's approval rating dipped slightly to 51%. Disapproval over housing policy jumped sharply, matching what this week's newspaper editorials focused on most.

This Week's Terms

Words in the News, Explained

Three terms that came up often in this week's national newspaper editorials — explained simply, so you can follow the news even if you're new to Korean politics and policy.

FilibusterPOLITICS

A way for lawmakers to delay a vote by speaking for as long as they legally can, without a time limit. It's a legal form of protest, but once the majority party votes to end debate, the bill still moves to a final vote.

Parliamentary InquiryLAW

A formal investigation that the National Assembly can launch into a specific issue, using its legal power to summon witnesses and demand documents. This week, ruling and opposition parties disagreed over whether to extend one looking into a local election vote-count scandal.

Dutch DiseaseECONOMY

A situation where a booming industry — like semiconductors — makes a country's other industries less competitive. The name comes from the Netherlands, where a 1970s natural-gas boom weakened its manufacturing sector.
