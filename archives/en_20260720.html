Editor's Letter

One Side Argues, the Other Argues Back

This issue finishes our two-part look at the June 3 local election in the Seorak region. The short version: the parties won, but the people lost. Opinion polls flipped in just two months, and in the same neighborhoods, voters chose one party for governor and a different party for mayor. The final result came down to the candidates themselves, not just their parties.

National newspapers this week kept arguing about a plan to remove prosecutors' power to demand more police investigation. Even inside the ruling party, some lawmakers want to keep this power and some want to remove it. A Gallup poll found that 61% of people want to keep it, and only 23% want to remove it — a result that connects directly to this week's newspaper debate.

The same day, the Ministry of National Defense announced a plan to merge the army, navy, and air force academies into one new academy. A separate poll found that more people oppose this merger than support it. This issue also asks readers whether Korea should introduce a carbon tax now, as summer heat waves continue.

This Week's Terms

Words in the News, Explained

Three terms that came up often in this week's national newspaper editorials — explained simply, so you can follow the news even if you're new to Korean politics and policy.

Supplementary Investigation PowerLAW

The power that lets a prosecutor order more police investigation when a case seems incomplete. Whether to remove this power from prosecutors is the biggest political argument in Korea right now.

Local Police-Prosecutor CollusionSOCIETY

A pattern where police or prosecutors who stay too long in one area become too close to local business and community leaders, making investigations weaker. Rotating staff between regions is one proposed fix.

Leveraged ETFECONOMY

A fund traded on the stock market that is designed to move two or three times as much as the index it tracks. Gains can be bigger, but so can losses — this has recently made stock market swings sharper.
