Editor's Letter

Same Event, Different Names — and Learning Together

Welcome back after the Chuseok holiday. All five national newspapers wrote about three things this week: the landmine blast in the DMZ, the new system that replaced the prosecutors' office on October 2, and the dispute over a Supreme Court nomination. On that dispute, a new NBS poll found that 55% think the chief justice should talk with the president again and nominate someone else, while 34% think the president should appoint the judge already nominated. In the same poll, 50% disapprove of the president's job performance, up 2 points.

Gallup Korea took a two-week break for Chuseok, so this issue uses the NBS poll instead. Gallup's next results come out on October 9. The saying we chose this week means "when names are right, words make sense." The papers argued about what to call the blast and where the border line really is.

This week's editorial is about learning. Because there is no university in Sokcho or the rest of the region, it argues that we need to work toward becoming a UNESCO Learning City, so citizens can learn from each other. In the region, Sokcho's summer tourism spending rose 7.6%, Goseong applied to become a Peace Economy Special Zone, and a new trail opened at Hangyesanseong Fortress in Inje.

County Notices

Autumn Leaves Traffic Plan, Festivals, and a New Trail

🏙️ Sokcho

🍁 Seoraksan traffic plan for autumn leaves

From October 3 to November 8, on weekends and holidays, cars may be stopped from entering the Sogongwon parking lot when it is crowded. Please park at the free B and C lots and take the free shuttle bus. Signs along the road show parking and cable car waiting times. Cars for people with limited mobility can still enter. Questions: Sokcho City Hall, 033-639-2000.

🕊️ Goseong

🐟 Goseong Pollack Festival

The 26th Goseong Pollack Festival runs until Monday, October 5, at Geojin 11-ri Beach. You can try hands-on fish catching, watch live cooking by chefs, ride a sea bus, and visit a navy ship. Check the county website for today's hours. Questions: Goseong County Office, 033-680-3000.

🏄 Yangyang

🍄 Book now for the Matsutake & Salmon Festival

The festival runs from October 16 to 18 along the Namdaecheon River. Book the mushroom hunt and the salmon catching (30,000 won each, 20,000 won on the first day) on the festival app, “GoGo Yangyang.” Book the mushroom liquor class (20,000 won) on the festival website. Part of the fee comes back as Yangyang local gift vouchers. Questions: Yangyang County Office, 033-670-2000.

⛰️ Inje

⛰️ New trail to Hangyesanseong Fortress

A 0.9 km trail from the visitor center parking lot to the fortress's south gate is now open. A round trip takes about one hour. It is open every day from 9 a.m. to 4 p.m. It closes when there is a wildfire warning. From 2027, you will need to book on the national park's online system. Questions: Inje County Office, 033-460-2000.

🌼 Autumn Flower Festival ends October 11

The Inje Autumn Flower Festival at Yongdae Tourist Site is open from 10 a.m. to 6 p.m. until Sunday, October 11. Questions: Inje County Office, 033-460-2000.

This Week's Terms

Words in the News, Explained

Three terms that came up often in this week's national newspaper editorials — explained simply, so you can follow the news even if you're new to Korean politics and policy.

Military Demarcation Line (MDL)SECURITY

The line between North and South Korea, drawn by the 1953 armistice. A 2 km-wide demilitarized zone lies on each side. Many of the posts that marked the line are gone, so where it lies became an issue again after this week's landmine blast.

Supplementary InvestigationLAW

Extra investigation of a case after the police send it on. Since October 2, prosecutors can no longer do this themselves. Instead, the new prosecution office asks the police to do it. Some worry that cases may go back and forth and take longer.

Commercial ReasonablenessECONOMY

A test of whether an investment can pay back its money and interest within the life of the project. Korea and the U.S. agreed to use this test for Korea's U.S. investments. It came up this week when the U.S. announced an Alaska gas project that Korea says it is only reviewing.
